HOW THE STAGE IS BUILT AND CHECKED
The kit assembles your scene and moves everything that changes from the solver data every frame. The runtime renders the stage at several times of the run and compares it with the solver timeline; anything of yours that moves, grows, appears or disappears by itself is rejected. Never compute physics yourself; all you draw is static, and every change goes through the bindings below.
1. Motion in space. A participant with a position (x/y, height, displacement, elongation) is a body in scene.bodies. The kit places it at the solver position in a measured frame fitted to the run, mirrors or turns it as declared, and draws axes, trails, vectors and labels as overlay asks; it also draws the support and string of a swinging body and the spring of a body on a spring. Bodies that interact along a line (a collision, a push, a body hitting a wall) share body.line; rigid ones are solid and may never pass through each other. Independent systems (several pendulums) are laid out side by side, each in its own frame: a fixed point that belongs to one is written "<id>:x,y" in a link, or with fixture.frame = id.
2. Things standing still at a physical place (wall, stop, pulley block, fixed charge, platform): scene.fixtures, placed in metres — preferably by the plan parameter that holds the position (xParameter, yParameter), turned by angleParameter, mirrored by flipBy (a parameter whose negative value reverses the drawing: polarity, field or current sense) — so they follow the sliders. Ropes, wires and rods whose ends move: scene.links.
3. Every other changing value is shown where it happens, in ONE drawing of the set-up: an instrument with its own svg (field "" when the drawing itself shows no value) that contains the objects in their real arrangement. Each observable is a moving part drawn ON that drawing (on = "0" or the drawing's field, svg ""), in the drawing's viewBox, at its object: charges travelling along the wire of its branch, a level rising in its vessel, a glow on its lamp, a needle on its meter. The drive maps the field to the part (rotate, translate, reveal, scale, opacity or travel); parts travelling on one drawing in one unit share one speed scale, so twice the current visibly flows twice as fast. Give each part a labelAnchor on its object: the kit prints the verified value there in a callout. A value with no place in the set-up gets its own small instrument; a set-up shown as a row of separate dials is wrong.
4. components: on the set-up drawing, one {object, box} per object named in observables (object = that name exactly, box = where it is drawn in viewBox units). The runtime checks that each box covers drawn artwork and that each observable's part or label lies on its object.
5. size: the share of the stage a drawing gets when there are several (1 = default); give the set-up the largest.
6. annotations name a component at a point of a drawing, optionally with a live value (field = exact solver key, "" for a name only).
7. Values outside observables (intermediate results) need nothing on the stage; the page lists every value under the canvas. Never write numbers in labels or artwork.

DESIGN (fill "design" first)

ARTWORK
- Each SVG is one complete document <svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="…"> in its own units, roughly 100–400 across (physical size is sizeMeters, never metres in the drawing); literal colours.
- Allowed: g, path, rect, circle, ellipse, line, polyline, polygon, defs, linearGradient, radialGradient, stop, clipPath, mask, pattern, filter (feGaussianBlur, feOffset, feDropShadow, feMerge, feFlood, feComposite), opacity, transform. Forbidden: script, style, image, foreignObject, use/href, animate*, external url(), text — every word comes from the kit.
- Make every object recognisable and finished: true proportions, two or three tones or a soft gradient for volume, one consistent outline weight, rounded joins. Wires, pipes and tracks are continuous strokes that really connect the components. Frame each drawing tightly, keep clear space around components for the callouts, and draw a moving part once, at its starting place, in its drawing's viewBox.
- All artwork together under 45000 characters.
